# Medium-Encounter Battle Map Collection

This collection comprises **30 larger fantasy encounter locations**, each designed as a **30 × 30-square tabletop battle map**. At 5 feet per square, every location presents a **150 × 150-foot** battle area, creating more room for flanking, multiple elevation zones, ranged engagement, moving hazards, and encounters involving several allied or opposing groups.

The visual direction extends the small-encounter collection: an orthographic overhead camera, richly textured painted fantasy terrain, even readable lighting, gridless artwork, and no embedded UI, labels, text, tokens, compass, creatures, or characters. Each map has broad connected lanes as well as clearly differentiated cover, difficult terrain, walls, doors, water, cliffs, or other decisive landmarks. Final images will be square and used beneath a virtual tabletop’s own 30 × 30 grid.

The package includes **five three-stage medium-scale encounters** and **25 standalone locations**, for **40 final images** across 30 encounter locations. Stage variants keep the camera and most primary landmarks fixed while altering the battlefield in ways that matter tactically.

## Stage swap design

| Sequence | Battlefield change |
| --- | --- |
| Stormwatch Keep | The breached gate opens an invasion route, while the final stage adds edge fires and collapses to force movement through the inner bailey. |
| Flooded Harbor | Calm low tide shifts to submerged quays and then a storm-flooded network of docks, rooftops, and boat routes. |
| Sinking Jungle City | Monsoon rain fills the canals, then much of the plaza becomes a mosaic of raised ruins and water lanes. |
| Great River Bridge | A central span fails, creating rubble cover and separated banks before burning barricades reshape the remaining approaches. |
| Glacial Vale | A fresh avalanche creates snow obstacles, then a warm thaw breaks the ice river and forms meltwater channels. |

> The maps will be intentionally gridless. In a VTT, align each image to 30 squares across and 30 squares down, then set the distance scale to 5 feet per square.
