# The Shattered Accord
## Campaign World Bible

> **Campaign premise:** A Gaelic-inspired high-fantasy world in which dragons and mortals are connected through two very different traditions: **Dragon Riders**, who are chosen by dragons, and **Dragon Warriors**, who wield power through dragon gems. A hidden enemy is exploiting the mistrust between the two groups while rediscovering the forbidden art of smashing dragon gems and forging weapons from their remains.

---

# 1. Campaign Identity

## Name
**The Shattered Accord**

## Setting
A high-fantasy world of mist-covered islands, ancient forests, storm-beaten coasts, high valleys, dragon-haunted mountains, and old kingdoms whose legitimacy is tied to dragons.

## Core Themes
- Trust versus suspicion
- Inherited power versus chosen bonds
- The cost of power
- Family and lineage
- Dragons as people rather than mounts or monsters
- Political misunderstanding
- Ancient traditions being weaponized
- The difference between possessing power and deserving it
- Unity between people who have been taught to mistrust one another

## Central Conflict
Two great traditions exist in an uneasy balance:

### Dragon Riders
Dragon Riders are **chosen by dragons**. A bond cannot simply be demanded or inherited. The circumstances of a bonding vary wildly: courage, kindness, shared danger, curiosity, music, healing, mutual purpose, or something known only to the dragon.

### Dragon Warriors
Dragon Warriors wield **dragon gems**. A gem may be:
- Taken from a dragon's hoard.
- Given voluntarily by a dragon.
- Passed through a family lineage.
- Entrusted by a temple or institution.
- Held as a symbol of royal legitimacy.

Many royal and noble houses have inherited gems for generations.

The two traditions have coexisted, competed, and occasionally fought throughout history.

# 2. World History

## The Age of Ceò
The oldest remembered age is called the **Age of Ceò**, the Age of Mist.

The world was wild, saturated with magic, and dominated by dragons. Great dragons occupied mountains, islands, forests, coastlines, and places where the boundaries of the world felt thin.

Mortals lived beneath their shadow.

Over time, mortals developed two fundamentally different relationships with dragonkind.

Some were **chosen**.

Others discovered that certain gemstones within dragon hoards carried extraordinary power.

These became the foundations of the Dragon Riders and Dragon Warriors.

## Rise of the Dragon Riders
A Dragon Rider is not created through ceremony alone. The dragon chooses.

Some bonds arise instantly. Others take years.

A Rider and dragon may share:
- Emotion
- Intent
- Senses
- Magic
- Instinct
- Dreams
- Limited telepathy

The bond is considered sacred by the Dràgonan Aontachd.

## Rise of the Dragon Warriors
Dragon gems became symbols of extraordinary authority and martial power.

Some warriors stole gems from dangerous hoards. Others were gifted gems. Powerful houses eventually began passing gems through generations.

Royal families became especially associated with inherited gems, and possession of a famous gem could become almost inseparable from a family's political legitimacy.

## The Old Harmony
There were periods in which Riders and Warriors operated together.

Dragon Riders often became scouts, emissaries, explorers, and guardians of wild places.

Dragon Warriors became defenders, champions, rulers, and keepers of ancient heirlooms.

Neither tradition was inherently good or evil.

## The Dragon Wars
Control of gems, questions of legitimacy, old injuries, and competing philosophies eventually brought periods of open conflict.

These wars reshaped borders and left both traditions suspicious of the other.

The modern peace is real, but fragile.

# 3. The Forbidden Art of Gemforging

## The Legend
An old and nearly forbidden legend claims that a dragon gem can be **smashed** and its remains forged into weapons, armor, or artifacts.

The results are extraordinary.

The cost is horrifying.

When a true dragon gem is deliberately destroyed:

1. The dragon connected to the gem dies.
2. The person who possesses or is spiritually bound to the gem dies.
3. A violent magical discharge is released into the world.
4. The fragments retain a concentrated and corrupted form of the gem's original power.
5. Those fragments can be forged into artifacts of tremendous strength.

The process is generally treated as myth, taboo, or propaganda from older wars.

It is real.

## Why This Matters
The rediscovery of Gemforging is the central hidden engine of the campaign.

Those using it are not merely stealing gems.

They are harvesting them.

---

# 4. Consequences of Gem Destruction

## The Weeping Sky
As more gems are destroyed, strange phenomena begin appearing across the world.

The best known emerging term is **The Weeping Sky**.

Possible manifestations include:
- Spectral lights resembling tears falling through the night sky.
- Unstable magic.
- Spells behaving unpredictably.
- Ley lines shifting.
- Unseasonable storms.
- Animal migrations becoming erratic.
- Dragons becoming restless.
- Riders experiencing shared nightmares.
- Dragon Warriors feeling phantom pain around destroyed gems.
- Places connected to destroyed gems becoming magically scarred.

The phenomenon eventually becomes one of the pieces of evidence showing Riders and Warriors that they are not simply attacking one another.

# 6. Major Factions

## The Dràgonan Aontachd
**Common translation:** Dragon Unity

### Identity
The most influential political and cultural organization associated with Dragon Riders.

### Governance
Traditionally led by a council of elder Riders chosen for wisdom, experience, and strength of bond rather than hereditary rank.

The council is sometimes called the **Seanair Council**.

### Philosophy
A dragon bond is sacred and cannot be reduced to ownership.

Dragons are allies, persons, and powers in their own right.

### Strengths
- Aerial warfare
- Scouting
- Fast communication
- Wilderness travel
- Dragon knowledge
- Diplomacy involving dragonkind

### Major Political Problem
As Dragon Warrior gems begin disappearing, suspicion increasingly falls upon the Riders.

The Riders are less concerned with possession of the stolen gems than with the accusation that they are responsible.

---

## The Cloch Custodians

### Identity
The most powerful institutional tradition of Dragon Warriors.

They are guardians, bearers, and inheritors of dragon gems.

### Homeland
Ard-Gleann.

### Governance
A hereditary monarchy supported by noble Dragon Warrior houses, military retainers, gem-bearing lineages, and advisers.

### Royal House
**House of Ailín**

### Culture
The Custodians see dragon gems as:
- Sacred trusts
- Symbols of duty
- Family legacies
- Weapons of last resort
- In some families, proof of rightful rule

### Political Crisis
The theft and destruction of Prince Daibhidh's gem destabilizes the royal house and gives enemies reason to believe the Cloch Custodians are vulnerable.

# 8. Religion

## The Order of the Draconic Covenant

### Role in the Setting
A major religious tradition suitable for clerics, paladins, knights, pilgrims, and religious NPCs.

### Core Beliefs
#### Harmony with Nature
Dragons embody ancient forces of the world and should not be treated merely as weapons.

#### The Sacred Bond
A voluntary bond between dragon and mortal is a reflection of the ideal relationship between power and responsibility.

#### Balance of Power
Power must be exercised in service of balance, protection, and stewardship.

### Practices
- Offerings of gems and metals to dragon hoards
- Planting sacred groves
- Pilgrimages to old dragon lairs
- Visiting battlefields associated with legendary dragons
- Meditation upon elemental forces
- Vows of protection

### Paladins
Paladins of the Covenant commonly swear to:
- Protect sacred sites
- Defend dragons from exploitation
- Recover stolen gems
- Protect innocent settlements
- Prevent magical desecration

# 9. Dragon Ecology

Dragons are not all legendary beings.

The world contains common dragons, regional dragons, hoarding dragons, bonded breeds, and extremely rare ancient creatures.

A dragon may:
- Maintain a hoard
- Have no interest in wealth
- Collect objects for sentimental reasons
- Collect knowledge
- Maintain an inherited hoard
- Have a dragon gem without revealing it to its Rider

This last fact becomes important when attacks begin occurring in Rider territories.

# 14. The Hidden Enemy

## The Veil of Thorns

> **GM SECRET — DO NOT PRESENT AS COMMON PLAYER KNOWLEDGE EARLY IN THE CAMPAIGN**

The Veil of Thorns is the hidden force manipulating the conflict between the Dragon Riders and Dragon Warriors.

### Public Knowledge
Almost none.

At the beginning of the campaign, neither great faction recognizes the Veil as the cause of the escalating conflict.

### Strategy
The Veil:
- Steals dragon gems.
- Destroys selected gems.
- Forges artifacts from the fragments.
- Searches dragon hoards for additional gems.
- Attacks Rider territories.
- Leaves evidence that encourages each side to blame the other.
- Exploits political chaos in Ard-Gleann.

### Objective
Their exact final objective can remain mysterious for much of the campaign.

At minimum they seek:
- Concentrated dragon power
- Gem fragments
- Magical weapons
- Destabilization of both Riders and Warriors

# 15. Morcant

## Name
**Morcant**

## Title
**The Shadowed Sovereign**

## Affiliation
Leader of the Veil of Thorns.

## Central Hypocrisy
Morcant condemns the traditional use of dragon gems while secretly possessing one himself.

## Gem
**The Lumina Shard**

The Lumina Shard is associated with light, clarity, revelation, purification, and radiant magic.

Morcant's evil comes not from the gem itself but from what he chooses to do with it.

## Powers of the Lumina Shard

### Illumination Manipulation
Morcant bends light, allowing him to:
- Blind opponents
- Create false images
- Conceal himself
- Distort what witnesses believe they saw

### Purifying Flame
Radiant flame capable of disrupting:
- Enchantments
- Defensive spells
- Magical wards
- Magical objects

### Insight of the Shard
Allows Morcant to:
- Penetrate many illusions
- Detect deception
- Identify weaknesses
- Perceive magical structures

### Healing Reversal
He can corrupt the healing nature of the gem and suppress recovery from wounds.

### Radiant Chains
Bands of concentrated light restrain targets and interfere with magical abilities.

## Character Concept
Morcant believes power is justified by the person capable of taking and using it.

He does not believe his own possession of a gem contradicts his philosophy.

In his mind, it proves it.

# 36. Dragon Rider Gathering — Early Campaign Scene

## Location
Eilean na Dràgon.

## Purpose
Introduce:
- Aeliana
- Fianna
- Seraphina
- Liora
- Their dragons
- Political tension among the Riders

## Important Tone
The Riders are **not primarily worried that gems are being stolen**.

They are worried that Dragon Warriors increasingly believe the Riders are responsible.

The meeting should not be a scripted movie scene.

Instead, the GM should establish:
- A tense gathering
- Rumors from Ard-Gleann
- Accusations being repeated publicly
- Younger Riders demanding action
- Older Riders cautioning restraint

Then allow the players to decide how their characters react.

## The Magical Event
During the gathering, Prince Daibhidh's Emerald Heart Gem is destroyed far away in Ard-Gleann.

The destruction is powerful enough to produce a visible or magical reaction that reaches Eilean na Dràgon.

Possible experience:
- Dragons suddenly scream.
- Every dragon in the gathering turns toward the same distant horizon.
- The air becomes painfully still.
- A pulse of green-white light crosses the sky.
- Some bonded Riders briefly feel a heartbeat that is not theirs stop.
- A crack of magic rolls across the horizon with no storm present.

The Riders do not know exactly what happened.

But the dragons know something has died.

This lets the players experience the gem destruction from a completely different location and perspective.

# 37. Opening Sequence

## Scene One — Caelum's Problem

### Situation
Caelum's Azure Flame Gem has been stolen.

### Truth
Ordinary thieves took it.

This is deliberately unrelated to the main conspiracy.

### Goal
Caelum seeks assistance from Prince Daibhidh.

This allows the campaign to begin with a relatively understandable problem before revealing something much larger.

---

## Scene Two — The Ambush

Prince Daibhidh and Caelum are traveling together after discussing Caelum's stolen gem.

The road becomes unnaturally quiet.

A deliberate obstruction blocks the path.

A coordinated group attacks.

The attackers are far more disciplined than ordinary bandits.

Their target is not Caelum.

Their target is the prince's **Emerald Heart Gem**.

The attackers escape with it.

### Key Mystery
Why take Daibhidh's gem when Caelum's already-stolen Azure Flame Gem is also missing?

Because the two thefts are unrelated.

The characters will initially assume otherwise.

---

# 38. Eirlys's Memory Before the Prince Returns

Before Daibhidh returns, Eirlys walks the halls of the royal residence and remembers her brother.

## Childhood Memory
A young Eirlys experiments with a mechanical bird in the castle garden.

Daibhidh brings her wildflowers and teases her affectionately.

Rather than praising her as a future warrior, he tells her that her mind and kindness may someday matter more to Ard-Gleann than any sword.

He asks her to promise that she will remain herself no matter what the expectations of their family demand.

She promises.

This memory should occur immediately before she learns he has returned injured and without his gem.

Its purpose is to make his death personal before it becomes political.

---

# 39. Prince Daibhidh's Death

## Location
Royal residence / castle in Ard-Gleann.

Daibhidh and Caelum return after the ambush.

Eirlys meets them.

Daibhidh explains that his gem was taken.

Before any meaningful recovery effort can begin, the stolen Emerald Heart Gem is destroyed somewhere far away.

## The Effect
A magical shock passes through the castle.

Daibhidh collapses.

Healing magic cannot repair what is happening because the injury is not physical.

Something fundamental connecting Daibhidh to the gem is being ripped apart.

Eirlys attempts to save him.

She cannot.

## His Last Moments
Avoid making his final words purely about plot objectives.

He should speak to Eirlys as her brother first and a prince second.

Possible final emotional idea:

> "You promised me once you would not let this place decide who you were."

Then, after struggling for breath:

> "Don't break that promise because of me."

His death leaves Eirlys with a command very different from "avenge me."

It gives her permission to become the ruler and person she chooses to be.

---

# 40. Simultaneous Event at Eilean na Dràgon

At approximately the same moment Daibhidh dies in Ard-Gleann:

The Dragon Riders witness the magical consequence.

They do not see Daibhidh.

They do not know his gem was destroyed.

They experience:
- A supernatural wave
- Agitated dragons
- A strange rupture in the sky
- A sensation of distant loss

This creates two parallel perspectives.

### Dragon Warrior Interpretation
A royal gem was stolen and destroyed.

The Riders are obvious suspects.

### Dragon Rider Interpretation
Something terrible has happened involving dragon magic.

Given the increasing accusations against them, some Riders may conclude the Warriors are experimenting with dangerous gem magic.

Both sides are wrong.

---

# 41. The Central Misunderstanding

The Veil's greatest weapon early in the campaign is not a forged sword.

It is interpretation.

Every event has just enough evidence to support the assumptions each faction already holds.

### Dragon Warriors See
- Missing gems
- Attacks involving dragons
- Rider mobility
- Rider opposition to inherited gem ownership

### Dragon Riders See
- Warriors weaponizing dragon power
- Gem-related magical catastrophes
- Political leaders demanding retaliation
- Armed expeditions entering dragon territories

Neither side sees the whole pattern.

The party eventually will.

# 42. Potential Major Twist — Origin of the Dragon Gems

> **OPTIONAL GM SECRET — NOT YET REQUIRED CANON**

An earlier concept proposed that all dragon gems are fragments of an ancient celestial dragon named **Auraxithar**.

This idea can remain optional until you decide whether you want it to be the campaign's deepest mythology.

## Potential Version
Auraxithar was an ancient celestial dragon betrayed and shattered by other dragons.

The scattered fragments of his essence became the first dragon gems.

If true, this would explain:
- Why gems seem alive
- Why destruction affects dragons
- Why gems can bond to mortals
- Why combining fragments might have world-changing consequences

## Caution
This twist fundamentally changes the meaning of dragon gems.

Keep it secret or non-canon until you decide you want the campaign to revolve around it.

# 44. Canon Snapshot

The following should currently be treated as the strongest established canon.

1. Dragon Riders are **chosen by dragons**.
2. Dragon Warriors gain power through dragon gems.
3. Gems may be inherited, stolen from hoards, or willingly given.
4. Royal houses frequently inherit gems.
5. Destroying a gem kills the connected dragon and its bearer and releases destructive magic.
6. Gem fragments can be forged into extraordinary weapons and armor.
7. The Veil of Thorns is secretly exploiting Gemforging.
8. Morcant leads the Veil and secretly possesses the **Lumina Shard**.
9. Eira serves Morcant while secretly bonded to **Lir**, a Mistral Shadow Dragon.
10. The Dragon Riders and Dragon Warriors initially believe the other faction is responsible for the escalating violence.
11. Caelum's **Azure Flame Gem** is stolen by ordinary thieves.
12. Caelum goes to Prince Daibhidh for help.
13. Daibhidh's **Emerald Heart Gem** is stolen in a separate organized ambush.
14. The Veil destroys Daibhidh's gem.
15. Daibhidh dies as a result.
16. Eirlys is Daibhidh's sister, an artificer, Dragon Warrior by lineage, and exceptional animal handler.
17. Eirlys possesses the inherited **Eclipse Shard** and has developed a relationship with **Nythendra**, a near-eternal dragon.
18. Aeliana is a Gloom Stalker Dragon Rider bonded to **Lysanthir**.
19. Aeliana is intended to become Caelum's love interest.
20. Lysanthir may eventually give **Aurora's Heart** to Caelum.
21. Fianna is a charismatic, sweet, fire-happy mage bonded to **Pyraethus**.
22. Seraphina is a bard whose song accidentally healed and attracted **Melodis**.
23. Kaelan is a monk whose temple awards the **Celestial Shard** to the winner of a trial every five years.
24. Liora is a deliberately powerful rogue with paired daggers, bonded to the green dragon **Virentia**.
25. Virentia may eventually give Liora her gem.
26. The destruction of Daibhidh's gem is experienced magically in distant Dragon Rider territory, helping introduce the Rider PCs while keeping the two locations separate.
27. The campaign begins amid political suspicion rather than open knowledge of the Veil.

# 46. Tone Guide for the GM

The setting works best if no major faction is stupid.

The Riders have reasonable evidence.

The Warriors have reasonable evidence.

The Veil succeeds because it understands history, prejudice, fear, and incentives.

Avoid making every clue immediately point toward a mysterious cult.

Instead, let discoveries first create **better questions**.

The dragons should also have their own opinions.

Some hate Dragon Warriors.

Some respect them.

Some willingly give gems.

Some consider that practice horrifying.

Some see Riders as companions.

Others see Riders as useful mortals.

The world becomes more believable when even the dragons disagree about what the relationship between dragon and mortal should be.

---

# 47. Campaign Opening Principle

The story should initially feel like it is about:

> **Who is attacking whom?**

Then gradually become:

> **Who benefits from making us believe that?**

And eventually:

> **What has our entire world misunderstood about dragon power?**

That progression is the heart of **The Shattered Accord**.
